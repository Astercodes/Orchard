How the tools connect

From “I want to try”
to “Look what I did.”

Say your child wants to learn a song on guitar. Here’s how one interest can travel through Orchard, from the first conversation to a memory worth keeping.

  1. Let them choose the goal

    Write their choice in the Monthly Goal Sheet: play the chorus of a song they love by the end of the month.

  2. Find a repeatable moment

    Agree on ten minutes after dinner twice a week. Use the Habit Tracker to remember when they practiced.

  3. Ask what’s getting easier

    At the Weekly Check-In, ask them to show you the part they figured out. If one chord keeps getting in the way, make that the next small goal.

  4. Keep something from the experience

    Save a note about the first time they played all the way through. Years later, that detail can help tell the story of how they learned to stick with something.

The review rhythm

Make time
to look back.

A quick conversation can change next week’s plan. A longer review helps you see what’s shifted over a term or a year.

  1. Weekly

    Check in

    Ask what felt good, what was hard, and what support would help next week.

  2. Monthly

    Reset priorities

    Review goals and habits. Choose what to keep, adjust, or let go.

  3. Quarterly

    Look for patterns

    Step back across the ten life areas and choose where to focus next.

  4. Annually

    Tell the story

    Gather the milestones and observations that capture who your child was this year.

From birth to eighteen

The tools change
as they grow.

The same ten life areas stay in view, while what you track and what your child takes on can change with age.

Early years

Notice the firsts

Keep health and food records handy, find a rhythm for mornings and evenings, and write down the milestones you want to remember.

School years

Build habits together

Choose one small goal at a time. Make space for reading, friendships, and interests, then ask what’s working at your next check-in.

Teen years

Hand over more of the plan

Let them lead parts of the review, set a savings goal, explore future paths, and make private space to reflect in their own words.

Getting started

Make it fit
your family.

Start with one thing your child wants to change or try. Keep what helps and revisit it together.

See where to start
Do we need a goal in all ten life areas?
One or two priorities are enough for the month. Keep the other areas in view so you can notice when your child’s needs or interests shift.
Where should we begin?
Pick a goal that matters to your child. Choose an action small enough to repeat this week and agree when you’ll talk about it.
What if the habit doesn’t stick?
Use your check-in to find out what happened. You can make the action smaller, change when it happens, or choose a different goal together.
How do the tools work together?
Use a goal to choose a habit, then bring what you noticed to your next check-in. The review helps you decide what to keep or change.
